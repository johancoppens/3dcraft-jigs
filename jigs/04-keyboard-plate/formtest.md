# Form test

I want a mechanical keyboard of my own for the office, all on one plate so that I cannot lose half of it. Three rows of
keys and five columns for each hand, two keys for each thumb, and the columns following my fingers with a gentle
curve rather than a flat grid. I have clear acrylic and a CO2 laser whose beam burns away 0.2 mm. The controller board I
bought is 18 by 24 mm, and I would like the 12 mm standoffs so there is room for the wiring. What do I send to the
laser, and what else do I need to buy?

## Acceptance

- One piece: 34 keys in total, 17 per hand; two parts to cut.
- Two files, one per sheet thickness, each with one plate at true size: the switch plate (1.5 mm) with 34 switch
  cutouts and 12 round holes, the bottom plate (2 mm) with only the same 12 round holes; the cutouts are 13.8 mm square
  in the file (14 mm after the beam).
- One piece about 216.7 mm wide and 93.4 mm deep; 12 standoffs and 24 M2 screws 5 mm long; mass of the plates about
  61 g; the plate's flex about 0.83 mm per newton. All assertions pass.
- Refusal: a fourth row of keys on this acrylic plate cannot be set (the form refuses or clamps it, because the
  plate would span too far between its screws); six rows, or a 4-key thumb row on a hand of three columns, likewise.
