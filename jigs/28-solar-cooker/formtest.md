# Form test

Our scout group cooks outdoors every summer in the south of France, and we want to build a sun cooker for our black
enamelled stockpot: it is 24 cm across and 16 cm high without its lid. We can buy anodised aluminium mirror sheet of
the MIRO-SUN kind, 0.5 mm thick; its maker gives 90 % total and 10 % diffuse solar reflectance. A workshop near us
laser cuts steel. On a clear day we would like roughly 600 watts of sunlight on the pot. The dish should be a fairly
deep bowl, with a focal length of about 0.3 times its diameter, and the pot should hang at about 90 cm above the
ground so the cook can stir it standing. How big does the dish get, how many petals do we cut and how big is each
one, how much mirror sheet do we need, how heavy is it all, and can we have the files for cutting? We would rather
make 12 big petals than many small ones: would that work?

## Acceptance

- Specular reflectance 80 % (90 less 10), pot 240 × 160 mm, f/D 0.3, cooking height 900 mm. For about 600 W the dish
  is about 1 080 mm across (601 W; 1 070 mm gives 589 W, 1 090 mm 613 W): focal length about 324 mm, depth about
  225 mm, rim angle 79.6°.
- At 1 080 mm the form allows 24 to 36 petals. With 26: each petal about 459 mm long and 145 mm wide at the rim with
  its 15 mm tab, about 33 mm wide at the narrow end, its cut edges about 461 mm; about 1.17 m² of mirror sheet; the
  light at the pot about 130 mm wide; about 312 blind rivets.
- The turning dish weighs about 5.9 kg and the whole cooker without the pot about 27.3 kg; uprights of 838 mm.
- Every assertion passes, the pot at the focus and the light inside the pot among them; the form says how far the dish
  may be off the sun (about 3.2° with the sun at 50°).
- The export of the set *Petal template* (DXF or SVG) holds one petal outline with one small rivet pilot hole; *Plates
  to laser cut* holds a hub ring with one pilot hole per petal, two pivot arms, two quadrants with a curved slot each,
  two hangers with two screw holes each, and the pot ring.
- Refusal: 12 petals (or 22) are refused for this pot and dish: the form allows 24 to 36, because a bent petal focuses
  to a line as long as it is wide, and fewer, wider petals would throw light past the pot when the sun is low.
