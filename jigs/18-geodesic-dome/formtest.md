# Form test

Our village allotment society wants a timber-strut geodesic greenhouse. We have a level circle of paving for it and we need about
38 square metres of floor. We have read that the 3V dome called three-eighths is the classic low one, so we would like
that. The struts will be sawn from 38 by 140 mm spruce, which we can get locally, and the hubs are steel plates a
local workshop can laser cut. Before we order anything we want to know how many struts of each length to saw, how
much timber that is in total, how many hubs of each kind, how high the dome gets, and we want the file for the laser
cutter. We also wonder whether we could have the same dome with 120 square metres of floor from cheaper 38 by 89 mm
timber.

## Acceptance

- The dome is 3V, 3/8 sphere: 120 struts on 46 hubs (A 30, B 40, C 50 struts; 15 four-way, 6 five-way and 25 six-way
  hubs). Sized for a 38 m2 floor, the radius is about 3.54 m and the floor about 6.96 m across.
- The saw lengths, over the long points of the mitres, are about 1.131 m, 1.324 m and 1.356 m for A, B and C, and the
  timber about 155 m in total. To cut by laser: 240 fin plates, 92 hub cap plates and 15 anchor plates. The dome's top is
  about 3.3 m above the ground, on the low ring; 5 base hubs stand about 56 mm higher than the other 10.
- The form also gives the mitre angle (about 27.3 degrees), the kerf (7 mm wide with the 5 mm plate, about 200 mm long),
  the hub tube (60.3 x 4 mm, 170 mm long), M10 bolts to order 60 mm long, and fin angles from about 55.7 to 72 degrees.
- The laser export (set *Hub plates to laser cut*, DXF or SVG) holds three fin outlines, one round cap and one anchor
  plate, at true size, each fin with two bolt holes.
- Refusal: 120 m2 of floor from 38 x 89 mm timber is refused: the form limits the floor to about 70 m2 at that section,
  because the longest strut would be too slender about its thin side.
