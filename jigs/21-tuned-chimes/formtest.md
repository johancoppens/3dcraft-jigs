# Form test: tuned-chimes

"Our pergola needs a wind chime and I already have three metres of ordinary aluminium tube at home, 25 mm across with
a 1.5 mm wall. I would like six tubes playing the friendly five-note scale that starts on the C an octave above middle
C, hanging with their tops all level, and it should begin to ring in a breeze of about 3 metres a second. How long do
I cut each tube, where do I drill it, how far out of tune will each one be if I cut exactly what you tell me, and how
much does the metal weigh? Is three metres enough? I have a tuner app: how do I use it so the set really comes out in
tune, and what do I do if my first tube rings 10 cents sharp? I want something I can print at full size to mark and
drill from, without having to pick anything out of a drawing. And what would change if I bought brass tube instead,
or wanted a glockenspiel with flat bars?"

## Acceptance

- The wind chime with a major pentatonic scale from C, octave 5, six notes, aluminium, 25 x 1.5 mm, tops level, wind
  3 m/s.
- Lengths C5 533.5, D5 503, E5 474.5, G5 434.5, A5 409.5, C6 375 mm (523.3 to 1046.5 Hz, each frequency an output);
  hole distances from the top end 119.5, 113, 106.5, 97.5, 92, 84 mm.
- Largest rounding error of the cut lengths about 1.2 cents, each note's shown; metal mass about 0.82 kg; stock about
  2 748 mm, so three metres is enough; the form states how far the real metal may be off before trimming (about
  13 cents for aluminium), the pitch change per mm trimmed, and a sail of about 50 x 70 mm for 3 m/s.
- The tuner: cut one tube to the test piece length (533.5 mm), undrilled, hang it in a loop at 22.4 %, read it against
  C5 and enter the reading. At +10 cents every length grows (C5 about 535 mm) and the 533.5 mm piece is trimmed to D5's
  new length (about 504.5 mm), with C5 cut again; three metres still suffice.
- The named set *Cut sheet* exports as SVG or DXF without picking: one full-size strip per note with its frequency,
  cut length and hole distance, and the outlines of the oak disc and the sail.
- Brass tube changes every length (shorter than aluminium, because the speed of sound in brass is lower: the longest
  about 448 mm, the shortest 314 mm, and a much larger spread of about 43 cents); a glockenspiel builds bars on a frame
  with two holes at each bar's nodes and the frame's pin holes on the sheet.
- Refusals: more than eleven notes of this scale (two octaves) cannot be set, and neither can an octave outside 3 to 6
  for this tube and these six notes, because the shortest tube would be too short to drill or the longest too long to
  handle; a reading beyond 100 cents is refused (the form says how to read a tuner that names the next note).
- All assertions that apply pass.
